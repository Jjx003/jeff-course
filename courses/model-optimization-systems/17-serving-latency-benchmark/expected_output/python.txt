=== Measuring latency without fooling yourself ===

--- Part 1: the percentile itself ---
nearest-rank percentiles of 1..1000: p50=500 p90=900 p99=990 p99.9=999
p99 of a 20-sample run: 20 (that is just the sample maximum)
smallest n where p99 is not the maximum: 100

--- Part 2: M/D/1 against Pollaczek-Khinchine ---
deterministic service 20.0 ms, 80000 arrivals per load, first 5000 dropped
rho=0.50  measured mean wait  10.093 ms   P-K  10.000 ms   ratio 1.009
rho=0.70  measured mean wait  22.960 ms   P-K  23.333 ms   ratio 0.984
rho=0.90  measured mean wait  99.696 ms   P-K  90.000 ms   ratio 1.108

--- Part 2b: twenty short benchmarks of the same server ---
rho=0.90, 1000 requests per run, 20 seeds: mean wait ranges 50.7 to 119.7 ms (2.4x spread)
long-run truth (P-K): 90.0 ms
(near saturation, a short benchmark measures which busy periods you
 happened to catch, not the server)

--- Part 3: the load-latency curve (prefill + tokens x ITL) ---
service = 40.0 ms prefill + tokens x 5.0 ms, tokens uniform 16..256 -> mean 720.0 ms

(the mean has a closed form; the tail does not - you have to measure it)

--- Part 4: coordinated omission ---
target load: rho=0.98 -> 1.36 req/s on a 1.39 req/s server
open loop   (arrivals keep coming): ttft p50   12152.2 ms   p99   50772.7 ms
closed loop (16 clients, think 11.04 s): ttft p50    1226.6 ms   p99    5232.9 ms   (1.22 req/s achieved)
closed-loop p99 understates open-loop p99 by 9.7x
(each waiting client stops sending, so the load generator backs off
 exactly when the server struggles - the missing requests are the slow ones)
