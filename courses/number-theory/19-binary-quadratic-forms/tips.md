- **Matrix Determinant:** The condition $\det M = \alpha\delta - \beta\gamma = 1$ ensures that the transformation is invertible over the integers ($\text{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$). If the determinant were $-1$, it would be an "improper equivalence", which flips the orientation.
- **Class Number 1:** The most beautiful cases are when the class number $h(\Delta) = 1$. This means *every* form of discriminant $\Delta$ is equivalent to the principal form. For example, $\Delta = -4$ has $h(-4) = 1$, and its unique reduced form is $x^2 + y^2$.
- **Heegner Numbers:** There are only nine imaginary quadratic fields with class number 1. The discriminants are $-3, -4, -7, -8, -11, -19, -43, -67, -163$. These are intimately related to Ramanujan's constant $e^{\pi \sqrt{163}} \approx 262537412640768743.99999999999925$, which is mysteriously close to an integer.
