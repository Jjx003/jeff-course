# Study Tips & Recap

### Recap

- **Divisibility:** $a \mid b$ means $b = ak$ for some integer $k$. Avoid thinking in fractions like $b/a$; think in terms of integer multiplication.
- **Transitivity:** If $a \mid b$ and $b \mid c$, then $a \mid c$.
- **Linear Combinations:** If $a$ divides two numbers, it divides any linear combination of them. If $a \mid b$ and $a \mid c$, then $a \mid (bx + cy)$.
- **Division Algorithm:** For any integer $b$ and positive integer $a$, $b = aq + r$ uniquely, with $0 \leq r < a$.

### Proof Strategies

When asked to prove that $x \mid y$:
1. Write down what you *know* using the definition: $x \mid z \implies z = x \cdot k$.
2. Write down what you want to *show*: $y = x \cdot (\text{some integer})$.
3. Use algebraic substitution and factorization to bridge the gap.
