- **Brahmagupta's Identity** is simply the norm of complex numbers! Let $z = a + bi$ and $w = c + di$. Then $|z|^2 = a^2 + b^2$ and $|w|^2 = c^2 + d^2$. The identity is just the statement that $|zw|^2 = |z|^2 |w|^2$.
- Similarly, **Euler's Four-Square Identity** arises from the multiplicative norm of **quaternions**, an extension of complex numbers with three imaginary axes $i, j, k$.
- **Sums of Three Squares:** Legendre proved that $n$ can be expressed as the sum of three squares if and only if $n$ is not of the form $4^a(8b + 7)$ for non-negative integers $a, b$. This proof is significantly harder than the two-square or four-square theorems!
