# Introduction to $p$-adic Numbers

In our journey through number theory, we've often worked with congruences modulo $p^k$. As $k \to \infty$, we might ask: is there a limit? Can we treat these infinite systems of congruences as a single "number"?

The **$p$-adic numbers** ($\mathbb{Q}_p$) provide the answer. They are an alternative completion of the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$, different from the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$. While $\mathbb{R}$ is built by completing $\mathbb{Q}$ with respect to the standard absolute value $|x|$, $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is built using the **$p$-adic norm** $|x|_p$.

In this module, we will:
1. Define the $p$-adic valuation and norm.
2. Construct $\mathbb{Q}_p$ as the completion of $\mathbb{Q}$.
3. Explore the "non-Archimedean" geometry of $p$-adic space.
4. Revisit Hensel's Lemma in its natural habitat.

### Why $p$-adic?
$p$-adic numbers are not just a curiosity; they are central to modern number theory, including the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem. They allow us to use tools from calculus and analysis to solve problems in discrete number theory.
