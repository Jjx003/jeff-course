# Offers, Negotiation, and Deciding

The surprising thing about the job search is how much work remains **after** you get an offer.

There is an extended period of team-matching conversations, lunch visits, recruiter calls, and an overwhelming volume of email you feel guilty about not answering. And unlike interviews, this part cannot be conquered by studying — there is no equivalent of grinding LeetCode for negotiation.

What you can do is be deliberate, and most people are not.

## Two things worth internalizing

**Initial offers leave room by design.** Recruiters routinely say some version of *"I don't expect you to take our first offer."* That is not a trap; it is an invitation to a normal, expected part of the process. Not negotiating is leaving money on the table that was placed there for you to take.

**You are outmatched, and that is fine.** The recruiter negotiates for a living and knows the market far better than you do. That asymmetry is real and it is not a reason to skip the conversation — it is a reason to prepare specifically, use your network for calibration, and script what you will say.

## The honest framing

Putting energy into this for a few weeks can be worth, literally, years of work at the initial offer. That is an uncomfortable sentence and it is arithmetic, not exaggeration.

And it remains true even if you would be happy with the first offer. Knowing your own values is good; declining to advocate for yourself is not the same thing.
