# The Checklist

**Before any compensation conversation:**

- [ ] Deflect the current-compensation question; do not anchor yourself
- [ ] Know the band and level structure, if the recruiter will share it
- [ ] Have two or three calibration data points from people you trust
- [ ] Know your walk-away, and your would-sign-immediately number
- [ ] Have written, verbatim, the sentences you will say

**On every written offer:**

- [ ] Base, bonus target, initial equity, sign-on
- [ ] Vesting schedule and cliff
- [ ] **Refresh policy** — often omitted, often large
- [ ] For private companies: strike price, preferred price, latest 409A, **total shares outstanding**
- [ ] Exercise window if you leave
- [ ] Level, and the criteria for the next one
- [ ] Start date flexibility

**Before deciding:**

- [ ] Met the actual manager
- [ ] Know the specific projects that exist right now, not the charter
- [ ] Understand compute and data access
- [ ] Understand the publication policy
- [ ] Written the story of your first year, for each offer

# Lines Worth Having Ready

**On current compensation:**
> "I would rather understand the role and the level first — I am happy to talk numbers once we both know what we are talking about."

**On making the ask:**
> "I am genuinely excited about this team and I want to make it work. Right now the offer is below X. If you can get there, I am ready to sign."

**On a competing offer:**
> "I have another offer at X. I would rather be here, and I am trying to get to a place where that is an easy decision."

**On a deadline you need extended:**
> "I have one more process finishing on the [date]. I want to give you a real answer rather than a rushed one — can we make the deadline the [later date]?"

**On being pushed for a commitment you are not ready to make:**
> "I am not able to commit today. What I can tell you is that if we get to X, I would accept."

# Traps

- **Not negotiating.** The room is there by design.
- **Naming a number first.** Deflect. If you must, name a range you would be delighted by, and know it functions as a ceiling.
- **Filling the silence.** State the ask, then stop. This is the most common self-inflicted wound in the whole process.
- **Negotiating without a competing offer or credible calibration.** You can, but timing your processes to overlap is worth real effort for exactly this reason.
- **Ignoring the refresh policy.** Over four years it often exceeds the sign-on.
- **Counting shares without knowing the total outstanding.** A share count alone is not information.
- **Treating the recruiter as an adversary.** They usually want to close you and need a justification to take upstairs. Give them one.
- **Deciding on compensation alone.** Manager, projects, compute, and publication policy determine whether you are still happy in two years.
