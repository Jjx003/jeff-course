# The Story Bank Template

Copy this and fill it in. One page per story, eight to twelve stories.

```
TITLE: (a two-word handle you can retrieve instantly)

SITUATION  (2 sentences: what, when, who, why it mattered)

TASK       (1 sentence: what was on you specifically)

ACTION     (4-6 sentences: what YOU did, and why. The decisions,
            the alternatives, the reasoning. This is the interview.)

RESULT     (2 sentences, at least one number. And what you learned
            or changed afterwards.)

ANSWERS:   [ ] disagreement    [ ] failure       [ ] hardest problem
           [ ] ambiguity       [ ] feedback      [ ] influence
           [ ] priorities      [ ] proud of

RISK:      (the follow-up you least want, and your honest answer)
```

That last line is the one people skip and the one that saves interviews. Every good story has a soft spot. Find it before they do.

# Timing

| Section | Target |
|---|---|
| Situation + Task | 20–25 seconds |
| Action | 60–75 seconds |
| Result + learning | 20–25 seconds |
| **Total** | **~2 minutes** |

# Traps

- **Preparing nothing** because you are obviously a reasonable person. This is the failure mode this module exists for.
- **Front-loading context.** Ninety seconds of setup and fifteen of action inverts the weighting.
- **"We" for everything.** They are hiring you, not your team. Say what *you* did.
- **A fake failure.** "I am too much of a perfectionist" reads as an unwillingness to be honest.
- **Blaming a collaborator.** Even when accurate, it is a strong negative.
- **No number in the result.** It makes every story sound vague.
- **A memorized script.** Prepare the beats, not the wording. Verbatim recitation sounds worse than mild imperfection.
- **Reusing one story for everything.** Interviewers compare notes across the loop.

# The Day Before

Reread the story bank once. Do not rehearse it again — at that point rehearsal only makes it sound more recited.

Then sleep. This is not filler advice: doing a technical interview on a few hours of sleep is a well-documented way to lose a loop over an off-by-one error that you would normally catch in seconds. Whatever you would cram in that hour is worth less than the sleep.

# Immediately After Every Interview

Within the hour, write down:

- Every question you were asked, as close to verbatim as you can manage.
- Where you hesitated or gave a weak answer.
- What the interviewer seemed to care about.
- Anything you learned about the team or the role.

This log is the highest-value study document you will build during the search, because it is calibrated to the companies you are actually talking to rather than to a generic list. After three loops it will be telling you exactly what to study next.
