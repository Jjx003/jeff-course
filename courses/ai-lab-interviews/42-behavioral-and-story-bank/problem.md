# The Behavioral Interview and Your Story Bank

This is the module people skip, and it is the one with the highest ratio of outcome to effort in the entire course.

Here is the failure mode, and it is common enough to be worth stating plainly: a technically excellent, genuinely collaborative person walks into a behavioral interview thinking *I am obviously well-behaved, this will be fine*, is asked "tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague", and comes up completely blank.

It is not a character flaw. **Reconstructing a hazy memory and narrating it well are two hard cognitive tasks, and doing both at once under evaluation is genuinely difficult.** The interview does not test whether you have good stories. It tests whether you can retrieve one in four seconds.

The fix is entirely mechanical: build the stories in advance so that during the interview you are **retrieving**, not **reconstructing**.

## What this interview actually measures

- **Judgment.** Did you make reasonable calls with incomplete information?
- **Collaboration.** Can you disagree without damage? Do you give credit?
- **Ownership.** Do you say "I" where you should, and "we" where you should?
- **Self-awareness.** Can you describe a failure without either minimizing or wallowing?
- **Occasionally: safety and impact.** AI labs sometimes add a question about societal consequences. Have a considered view.

## The question families

Almost every behavioral question is one of these eight:

1. Tell me about a time you disagreed with someone.
2. Tell me about a project that failed, or that you got wrong.
3. Tell me about the hardest technical problem you have solved.
4. Tell me about a time you had to make a decision without enough information.
5. Tell me about receiving difficult feedback.
6. Tell me about influencing someone without authority.
7. Tell me about competing priorities, or something you had to drop.
8. Tell me about something you are proud of.

Eight to twelve well-prepared stories cover all of them, because good stories map onto multiple questions.
