## How to practice

Read one company release and underline only the sentences that mention capacity,
lead time, customer concentration, margins, inventory, export rules, purchase
commitments, or product qualification. Then write a four-sentence thesis:

1. The scarce capability is...
2. The bottleneck appears to be...
3. The company captures economics through...
4. I would be wrong if...

## Concrete questions to ask

- What product generation is this claim about?
- Is the evidence about orders, shipments, deployments, or revenue?
- Does the same signal appear in at least one supplier and one customer source?
- Is the bottleneck a component, a manufacturing step, a system-integration
  step, a policy gate, or customer site readiness?
- Is the cited data fresh enough for the product cycle?
- Does the claim explain margins, or only revenue?
- What would you expect inventory, capex, or purchase commitments to do if the
  thesis is true?

## Source caveats

- Annual reports are authoritative but often lag the current bottleneck.
- Earnings calls are current but carefully messaged.
- Industry reporting is often early but can be stale or sample-biased.
- Government rules require exact dates and product scope.
- Capacity estimates should be treated as ranges unless a company filed or
  disclosed the number directly.
