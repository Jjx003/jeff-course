## Evidence to check

- HBM revenue growth, HBM mix, and management commentary by generation.
- Customer qualification language: sample, qualified, designed-in, shipping,
  allocated, or sold out.
- Stack height, bandwidth, and power/thermal claims for HBM3E and HBM4.
- Capex specifically tied to HBM, TSV/stacking, test, and advanced DRAM nodes.
- DRAM price trends outside HBM, especially server DDR.
- Whether AI customers reserve supply ahead of time.
- Packaging capacity updates from the accelerator supply chain.
- Customer concentration and second-source qualification.

## Analysis workflow

1. Start with the accelerator roadmap: which platforms need which HBM generation?
2. Estimate stack demand: accelerator units times stacks per accelerator.
3. Map qualified suppliers by customer and generation.
4. Check whether the bottleneck is memory, packaging, logic dies, substrates, or
   customer deployment.
5. Compare HBM capacity additions with verified commitments.
6. Tie the conclusion back to ASP, margin, and ordinary DRAM supply.

## Red flags

- "Sampling" is treated like volume revenue.
- A supplier's total DRAM scale is treated as proof of HBM share.
- Market share is quoted without saying whether it is revenue, bits, stacks, or
  qualified capacity.
- HBM growth is modeled without packaging constraints.
- Peak-cycle operating margin is capitalized as if it were permanent.
