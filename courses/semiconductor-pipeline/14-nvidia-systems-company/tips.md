## Evidence to check

- Latest NVIDIA annual report and quarterly releases.
- Data Center revenue versus Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive.
- Gross margin changes during Hopper-to-Blackwell and Blackwell-to-successor
  transitions.
- Mentions of Blackwell, Blackwell Ultra, GB200/NVL systems, Rubin, H20, export
  controls, supply constraints, and customer deployment timing.
- Cloud customer commentary from Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta, Oracle, and
  major GPU cloud providers.
- Memory supplier commentary on HBM generation, allocation, qualification, and
  profitability.
- TSMC and OSAT commentary on advanced packaging and capacity expansion.
- Networking commentary: InfiniBand, Spectrum-X Ethernet, optics, NICs, DPUs,
  and whether customers choose NVIDIA fabric or alternatives.

## Source-aware caveats

- Refresh the latest quarter before using any revenue or margin number. NVIDIA's
  fiscal year does not match the calendar year, so label fiscal periods clearly.
- Treat management performance claims as claims, not neutral benchmarks, unless
  you understand the workload and comparison point.
- HBM and CoWoS capacity estimates age quickly. Older pieces can explain the
  mechanism but should not be used as current capacity without updates.
- Cloud capex is not all GPUs. It can include buildings, power, networking,
  land, leases, CPUs, storage, and general infrastructure.
- Customer concentration can be economically positive and strategically risky at
  the same time.
- Export-control analysis must use exact rule dates, products, and geographies.
  Do not generalize from H20 to every China or non-China product.

2. List one demand signal, one supply signal, one deployment signal, and one

4. Name the strongest ASIC substitution risk and the strongest reason it may
   coexist with NVIDIA.
5. Identify one fact that would move you from bull to base, or base to bear.
