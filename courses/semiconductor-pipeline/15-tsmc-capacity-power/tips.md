## Evidence to check

- Annual revenue, net income, gross margin, operating margin, capex, and wafer
  capacity.
- Revenue by platform, especially HPC and smartphone.
- Revenue by node, especially 3nm, 5nm, 7nm, and mature/specialty nodes.
- Commentary on CoWoS, SoIC, InFO, photonics, and OSAT partnerships.
- Overseas fab ramp timing, subsidy terms, customer commitments, and margin
  commentary.
- Customer-side evidence from Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Qualcomm, cloud
  ASIC teams, and HBM suppliers.

## Questions to ask while reading sources

- Is management describing wafer demand, package demand, or total system demand?
- Are capacity additions backed by customer commitments or by broad market
  forecasts?
- Is advanced packaging still the bottleneck after announced expansions?
- Are overseas fabs strategic insurance, profit centers, or both?
- Is margin pressure temporary ramp cost, structural cost inflation, or
  underutilization?
- Which customer behavior would prove that Samsung Foundry or Intel Foundry has
  become a real second source?

## Red flags

- Precise CoWoS capacity claims with no primary source.
- Treating all 3nm or 5nm wafers as economically identical.
- Assuming AI demand automatically means higher TSMC margin.
- Ignoring HBM availability when forecasting AI accelerator shipments.
- Calling overseas capacity "duplicative" without modeling resilience value.
- Using customer press releases as proof of high-volume foundry share shift.

## Source caveats

Use official annual reports and earnings materials for audited history,
reported segment/platform trends, and management's own wording. Use specialist
reporting for bottleneck color, but mark it as external estimate unless TSMC or
the customer confirms it. Packaging capacity numbers are especially easy to
misread because sources may mix wafers, packages, interposers, monthly capacity,
or annualized run rate.
