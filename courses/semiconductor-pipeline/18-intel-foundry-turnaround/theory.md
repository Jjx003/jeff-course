## Mini-model: foundry credibility

External customer trust depends on:

```text
credible PDK + yield + cost + packaging + IP/EDA + schedule discipline + confidentiality
```

Missing one term can sink a foundry bid. A great transistor is not enough if the
design ecosystem is immature or the customer's product schedule is at risk.

## Utilization economics

A leading-edge fab has high fixed costs:

$$
\text{unit cost} =
\frac{\text{depreciation} + \text{fixed operating cost}}{\text{good wafers shipped}}
+ \text{variable cost per wafer}
$$

This makes utilization central. When good wafer output rises, fixed cost is
spread over more units. When demand is weak or yield is low, losses can remain
large even if the technology is improving.

## Internal volume versus external proof

Internal volume helps because Intel can debug new nodes with its own products.
But external proof is different:

| Evidence | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Internal CPU ramps | Intel can use the node for its own roadmap | Merchant foundry customers trust it |
| Test chip tapeouts | PDK and process can support designs | Commercial volume is committed |
| Named customer tapeout | Customer engagement is real | Long-term wafer volume is large |
| Multi-year wafer commitment | Foundry economics may scale | Yield and margin will be attractive |

## 18A and 14A as different tests

18A tests whether Intel can restore leading-edge process credibility. 14A tests
whether customers believe enough in the roadmap to fund the next leg of the
capital cycle.

That difference matters because a foundry can have a technically impressive node
and still fail commercially if the ecosystem and volume do not follow.

## Leading versus lagging indicators

| Indicator | Usually leads or lags? | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| PDK release and EDA enablement | Leading | Customers need this before tapeout |
| External tapeout | Leading | Shows a customer is investing engineering effort |
| Tool install and capex | Leading | Shows capacity intent, but not demand proof |
| Product launch on Intel node | Mixed | Confirms some execution, often internal first |
| Foundry revenue | Lagging | Arrives after design, tapeout, yield, and ramp |
| Foundry operating loss | Lagging/mixed | Reflects utilization, depreciation, charges, and pricing |

## Disconfirming evidence checklist

The Intel Foundry bull case weakens if:

- 18A products slip or require unfavorable cost/performance tradeoffs.
- 14A lacks a significant external customer.
- Intel product teams choose external foundries for the highest-value tiles.
- Foundry losses narrow only because impairments fade, not because utilization
  improves.
- PDK/IP/EDA maturity trails customer schedules.
- Packaging wins do not translate into wafer-fab utilization.
- Government programs become the main demand source rather than a bridge to
  commercial customers.
