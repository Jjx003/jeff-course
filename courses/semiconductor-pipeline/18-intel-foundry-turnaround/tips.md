## Evidence to check

- Intel Foundry operating loss and why it changes.
- 18A product ramp evidence: named products, timing, yield commentary, and
  volume language.
- Named external foundry customers and wafer-volume commitments.
- 14A roadmap decisions and whether a significant external customer appears.
- PDK, IP, and EDA ecosystem milestones.
- CHIPS Act funding and secure manufacturing programs.
- Whether Intel product groups manufacture internally or outsource key tiles.
- Advanced packaging wins and whether they pull wafer volume with them.

## Analysis workflow

1. Split the thesis into technology proof, ecosystem proof, and economics proof.
2. For technology proof, track 18A yield, product ramps, and process milestones.
3. For ecosystem proof, track PDK maturity, IP availability, EDA support, and
   external tapeouts.
4. For economics proof, track utilization, depreciation, capex, and customer
   wafer commitments.
5. Ask whether government support is bridging to commercial demand or masking
   weak demand.
6. Look for disconfirming evidence from Intel's own product-sourcing choices.

## Red flags

- "Customer interest" is treated as a wafer-volume commitment.
- Internal products are used as proof that external customers will adopt the
  foundry.
- Foundry losses narrow without evidence that utilization improved.
- Packaging wins are counted as if they automatically fill leading-edge fabs.
- CHIPS funding is treated as a substitute for yield, cost, and customer trust.
- Roadmap milestones are discussed without PDK/IP/EDA readiness.
