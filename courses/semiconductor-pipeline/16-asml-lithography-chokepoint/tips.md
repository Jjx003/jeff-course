## Evidence to check

- EUV system units, EUV revenue, EUV bookings, and High-NA milestones.
- DUV immersion sales, dry DUV sales, and regional mix, especially China.
- Installed-base management revenue, field-option commentary, and service
  trends.
- Net bookings, backlog, shipment timing, and order-cancellation language.
- Foundry and memory customer capex plans.
- Export-control changes from the Netherlands, U.S., Japan, and the EU.
- Supplier-capacity commentary around optics, stages, light sources, and other
  critical modules.

## Questions to ask while reading sources

- Is ASML growth coming from new tools, richer tool mix, service, or upgrades?
- Are bookings broad-based or concentrated in one customer or pull-forward?
- Is China DUV demand structural, policy-driven, or inventory-like?
- Which customer capex plans actually require EUV rather than packaging,
  buildings, or non-lithography tools?
- Is High-NA being adopted for learning, pilot production, or high-volume
  manufacturing?
- Are supplier constraints limiting ASML shipments even when demand is strong?

## Red flags

- Treating EUV units and total ASML revenue as interchangeable.
- Assuming every AI datacenter dollar creates immediate EUV demand.
- Ignoring DUV normalization after China pull-forward.
- Counting backlog as risk-free revenue without checking timing and controls.
- Treating the first High-NA shipment as proof of broad adoption.
- Mapping a customer fab announcement to ASML revenue without checking whether
  the spend is building shell, tools, packaging, memory, or services.

## Source caveats

Use ASML annual reports and earnings releases for reported sales, bookings,
backlog, system units, installed-base management, and management framing. Use
customer capex commentary to infer demand direction, but mark the inference
clearly. Export controls can change licensing outcomes quickly, so avoid stale
assumptions about what can ship to China.
