## Thiol acidity and microenvironment

The Henderson-Hasselbalch relationship gives the fraction of cysteine that is
deprotonated:

$$
\frac{[R-S^-]}{[R-SH]} = 10^{pH - pK_a}
$$

If $pH = 7.4$ and $pK_a = 8.3$, the thiolate fraction is significant but not
dominant. If a protein lowers the cysteine pKa to 6.5, the thiolate fraction
becomes much larger at physiological pH.

That is why local environment matters. Nearby positive charge can stabilize
$R-S^-$, while burial in a nonpolar environment may either favor neutrality
or make a charged thiolate costly unless compensated.

## Redox language

Oxidation is loss of electrons. Reduction is gain of electrons. In a
disulfide bond, each sulfur is more oxidized than it was in the thiol state.

For proteins, redox chemistry is often coupled to small-molecule buffers:
glutathione, thioredoxin, and other systems maintain compartment-specific
redox states. The details vary by organism and compartment, but the high-level
rule is stable: cytosol tends to keep cysteines reduced; secretory/extracellular
spaces permit disulfide formation.

## Structural effect of a disulfide

A disulfide introduces a covalent crosslink:

$$
i \leftrightarrow j
$$

where residues $i$ and $j$ may be far apart in sequence but close in the
folded structure. This crosslink narrows the conformational search space.
It can:

- stabilize a local loop,
- connect two secondary-structure elements,
- tie together domains or chains,
- or lock an active conformation.

Because it is covalent, a disulfide has a larger structural consequence than a
single hydrogen bond. But it is not automatically beneficial: the geometry
must match the folded state.

## Folding and disulfide isomerization

If a protein has $n$ cysteines that all pair into disulfides, there are many
possible pairings. Only one pattern may be native. Wrong pairings can create
kinetic traps because the protein becomes covalently constrained before it has
reached the correct fold.

Protein disulfide isomerases solve this by catalyzing exchange reactions.
They can break and reform disulfides until the lower-energy native pattern is
found.

This is a preview of a recurring folding theme: stability and kinetics are
not the same thing. A native disulfide pattern can stabilize the final fold,
while incorrect disulfides can block the route to that fold.
