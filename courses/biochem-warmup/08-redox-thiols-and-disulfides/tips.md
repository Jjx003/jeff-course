## Study tips

- Memorize the cysteine pair: thiol $R-SH$ and thiolate $R-S^-$.
- Connect lower pKa with more thiolate at the same pH.
- Keep redox vocabulary straight: oxidation loses electrons; reduction gains
  electrons.
- Ask where the protein lives. Cytosol and extracellular space have different
  disulfide expectations.

## Common pitfalls

- **Disulfides are covalent.** They are not salt bridges or hydrogen bonds.
- **All cysteines are not equally reactive.** pKa and local environment tune
  reactivity.
- **Disulfides are not common everywhere.** They are enriched in secreted and
  extracellular proteins, not ordinary reducing cytosol.
- **More disulfides is not always better.** Incorrect pairings can create
  folding traps or destabilize the intended structure.

## Going deeper

- Compare cysteine protease active sites with structural disulfides.
- Look up protein disulfide isomerase and oxidative folding in the endoplasmic
  reticulum.
- When viewing a protein structure, find whether cysteine residues are reduced
  thiols, metal ligands, or paired disulfides.
