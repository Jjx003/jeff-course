## Enthalpy, entropy, and water release

The sign of a process is summarized by Gibbs free energy:

$$
\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S
$$

A process is favorable when $\Delta G < 0$. Hydrogen bonds often contribute
favorable enthalpy ($\Delta H < 0$), while releasing constrained water can
contribute favorable entropy ($\Delta S > 0$).

The hydrophobic effect is a useful example because it is often misunderstood.
Two nonpolar surfaces coming together may have weak van der Waals attraction,
but the larger payoff is frequently that water molecules near those surfaces
become less constrained when exposed area decreases.

## Hydrogen bonds must be replaced

A polar group in water can usually hydrogen-bond with water. If that group is
buried inside a protein, those water contacts are lost. For burial to be
favorable, the protein often needs to replace them with internal hydrogen bonds
or electrostatic contacts.

This is why unsatisfied buried polar atoms are energetically expensive. In
structure analysis, they are often red flags unless the environment is unusual.

## Surface area intuition

The hydrophobic contribution is often approximated as scaling with buried
nonpolar surface area:

$$
\Delta G_\mathrm{hydrophobic} \propto -\Delta A_\mathrm{nonpolar}
$$

where $\Delta A_\mathrm{nonpolar}$ is the amount of nonpolar area removed from
contact with water. This is only a model, but it explains why compact packing
matters.

## Protein-folding preview

Protein folding balances many terms at once:

- backbone conformational entropy resists folding.
- hydrophobic burial favors compact states.
- hydrogen bonds and salt bridges can stabilize specific geometries.
- steric clashes reject impossible arrangements.
- side-chain packing selects among compact alternatives.

Water is involved in every one of those terms because the unfolded and folded
states expose different surfaces to solvent.
