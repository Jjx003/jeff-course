## Noncovalent interaction check

Protein structure is held together by many individually weak interactions.
No single hydrogen bond or van der Waals contact explains a fold by itself;
specificity and stability come from networks of contacts that fit the local
environment.

![Map of noncovalent interactions in a binding pocket](/courses/biochem-warmup/noncovalent-interactions-map.svg)

This quiz checks the core ideas:

- hydrogen-bond donors, acceptors, distance, and geometry,
- salt bridges and dielectric screening,
- van der Waals packing,
- pi stacking and cation-pi interactions,
- desolvation costs,
- and why buried polar groups need partners.

Use the best chemical answer, not just the strongest-sounding interaction.
