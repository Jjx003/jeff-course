## Study tips

- Think of the backbone as rigid peptide planes connected by rotatable
  hinges.
- Associate $\phi$ with $N-C_\alpha$ and $\psi$ with $C_\alpha-C$.
- Do not memorize a Ramachandran plot before you understand its purpose:
  it is a map of steric feasibility.
- Treat glycine and proline as the two special cases you should recognize
  instantly.

## Common pitfalls

- **Peptide bonds are not freely rotating single bonds.** Resonance matters.
- **Planarity does not mean the whole protein is flat.** Peptide units are
  planar; the full chain bends through allowed torsions.
- **Proline is not just hydrophobic.** Its backbone ring constraint is often
  the more important feature.
- **Glycine is not merely small.** Its lack of a beta carbon opens backbone
  conformations other residues cannot easily adopt.

## Going deeper

- Look up example Ramachandran plots for glycine, proline, and general
  residues.
- Compare alpha-helix and beta-strand torsion-angle regions.
- When reading protein structures, watch where glycines and prolines occur:
  they often mark turns, tight loops, or helix disruptions.
