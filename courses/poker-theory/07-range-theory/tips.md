# Tips and Going Deeper

## Thinking Prompts

**When analysing a hand, always ask:**

1. *What range does villain's action represent?* — An UTG open-limp looks very different from an UTG open-raise.
2. *How does this board connect with each player's range?* — Before asking "what do I do?", ask "who does this board help more?"
3. *What are the strongest hands each player can have here?* — Nut advantage often determines who should be the aggressor.
4. *Does my bluffing hand hold blockers to villain's calling range?* — Ace-blocker bluffs are mechanically superior to king-high bluffs on ace-high boards.

## Common Mistakes

- **Hand-reading in absolutes** — "He must have AK" rather than "his range is 40% AK, 25% QQ+, 35% bluffs."
- **Ignoring board-to-range fit** — Betting the same size on A72r and JT9ss. Dry boards favour large bets by the aggressor; wet boards call for smaller sizes or checking back.
- **Forgetting your own blockers** — If you hold K♠Q♠ and the board is A♠J♠T♠, you block the K♠ combinations in villain's range. Their nuts (A♠X♠) are unblocked; your hand is a bad bluff.
- **Over-narrowing villain's range** — Making decisions based on what you *hope* villain has rather than their full plausible distribution.

## Going Deeper

- [PokerStrategy: The Hand Matrix Explained](https://www.pokerstrategy.com) — visual walkthrough of the 13×13 grid and how to read range percentages
- *Applications of No-Limit Hold'em* by Matthew Janda — the definitive text on range construction, range equity, and GTO fundamentals
- *Modern Poker Theory* by Michael Acevedo — solver-backed range analysis, including board texture categorisation and bet-size selection
- [GTOWizard Range Explorer](https://gtowizard.com) — interactive tool for viewing GTO ranges by position and action; free tier available
- [Flopzilla](https://flopzilla.com) — desktop range vs. board analysis tool; excellent for learning how flops interact with preflop ranges

## Mental Shortcuts for Live Play

- **UTG range = premiums only.** If an UTG player bets all three streets, assume they have it until the line no longer makes sense.
- **BTN range = wide.** They can have two pair with 97s on a 9-7-2 board; don't assume they can only have strong aces.
- **Wet boards = pump the brakes.** On JT9 rainbow, even if you have AA, you may be far behind a range that contains 87, Q8, 76, and two-pair combos.
- **Count blockers before bluffing.** Take five seconds to ask: "does my hand contain a card that reduces the combos of villain's calling range?" If yes, the bluff has extra EV.
