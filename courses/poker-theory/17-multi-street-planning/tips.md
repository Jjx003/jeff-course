# Tips: Multi-Street Planning

## Hints

1. **Before betting the flop, state your plan for the turn.** "I'm betting the flop. On a blank turn I will barrel again. On a flush-completing turn I will check." If you cannot complete that sentence, you don't have a plan yet — stop and think before acting. The act of articulating the plan reveals when a line is internally inconsistent (e.g. betting the flop with a hand that can't call a raise and can't barrel most turns).

2. **Classify your hand before acting.** Is this hand a bet-fold, bet-call, check-call, or check-raise situation? A hand that wants to pot-control (check-call) but accidentally bets ("I have top pair") will face an awkward decision on the turn with a bloated pot and no plan. Explicit classification takes two seconds and prevents a category of common mistakes.

3. **Over-barreling is the most common bluffing mistake.** Many players who learn about double-barrels and triple-barrels start firing every street with their entire bluffing range. But bluff combos are constrained by the MDF ratio at each street — not all bluffs can profitably continue. Give up on the turn with hands that have no equity and no improving potential when the turn card favours the caller's range. The correct barrel frequency is lower than intuition suggests.

4. **Implied odds are only real if you can collect them.** A flush draw justifies implied odds only if, when you hit, the opponent will pay off a river bet. If the board makes the flush obvious and the opponent will check-fold, implied odds are minimal. Implied odds are highest against opponents who call too wide, in deep-stacked situations, and when the completing card is not the most obvious draw on the board.

5. **River sizing: decide before you act.** On the river, determine your sizing before taking any action. Sizing tells are one of the biggest live tells: players bet large with value (wanting more) and small with bluffs (hoping for cheap folds). Standardise your river sizing by hand category so the opponent cannot read your holding from the bet amount alone. The standard approach is one size for polarised bets (75–100% pot) and a smaller size for thin value (40–60% pot).

## Going Deeper

- **[Applications of No-Limit Hold'em](https://www.amazon.com/Applications-No-Limit-Hold-em-Matthew-Janda/dp/1880685558)** — Matthew Janda. Chapters 9–12 cover multi-street play, barrel frequencies, and the mathematical basis for double- and triple-barrel decisions. The clearest treatment of the topic in print.
- **[Modern Poker Theory](https://www.amazon.com/Modern-Poker-Theory-Building-unbeatable/dp/1909457892)** — Michael Acevedo. Solver-based analysis of flop, turn, and river decision trees with frequency data for each hand category. Chapter-level breakdowns of specific board textures across multiple streets.
- **[GTO Wizard — Hand Analysis](https://www.gtowizard.com)** — Use the hand analysis tool to trace specific hands through multi-street lines and see the EV of each action at each decision node. Invaluable for understanding which turn/river cards change the plan.
- **[Run It Once](https://www.runitonce.com)** — Phil Galfond and other high-stakes professionals breaking down specific multi-street spots on video. Watching an expert explain a hand plan in real time accelerates the development of multi-street thinking faster than reading alone.
