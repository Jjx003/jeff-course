# Tips: Position and Initiative

## Hints

1. **Position is permanent on the post-flop streets.** The button is profitable not because of pre-flop advantage — everyone sees the same cards — but because it is always last to act on every post-flop street for the entire hand. When evaluating whether a pre-flop call is profitable, position is one of the most important factors. A marginal hand is often a clear call from the button and a fold from the small blind.

2. **OOP does not mean passive.** A common mistake is checking and calling OOP repeatedly with medium hands, giving the IP player free information and cheap equity realization. The OOP player has active weapons: the check-raise for strong hands and draws, the well-sized donk bet for specific board textures, and large OOP value bets that charge the IP player for calling. Passivity OOP is exploitable.

3. **The check-raise must be polarised.** If you check-raise with only your strongest made hands (sets, straights) and never with draws, a good opponent will fold everything but the absolute nuts against your check-raises — making them unexploitable *by you*. Check-raising draws and semi-bluffs (flush draws, open-enders) is what gives the check-raise its full power. Do not check-raise medium hands — those should check-call or check-fold.

4. **Initiative is about the range, not your specific hand.** You can c-bet a board you missed as part of a balanced range strategy, because your *range* maintains a statistical advantage even when your specific hand doesn't connect. Ask "what does my range do on this board?" before "what should I do with this specific hand?" Range-first thinking prevents the mistake of always c-betting because "I raised pre-flop" and always checking because "I missed the flop."

5. **Equity realization should inform pre-flop calling decisions.** When facing a 3-bet OOP, consider how well your hand type realizes equity in this position. Suited connectors realize equity reasonably well even OOP because they make strong hands that justify building pots. Offsuit high-card hands ($K\diamondsuit T\clubsuit$) realize equity poorly OOP — they can't easily bluff multi-street and don't often make hands strong enough to stack off. Fold these more aggressively from out of position.

## Going Deeper

- **[Applications of No-Limit Hold'em](https://www.amazon.com/Applications-No-Limit-Hold-em-Matthew-Janda/dp/1880685558)** — Matthew Janda. Chapters on position, equity realization, and out-of-position play are mathematically rigorous and directly applicable to the concepts in this module.
- **[The Theory of Poker](https://www.amazon.com/Theory-Poker-Professional-Player/dp/1880685000)** — David Sklansky. The classic treatment of position, initiative ("the advantages of last action"), and free cards. Foundational reading that has stood the test of time.
- **[Poker's 1%](https://www.amazon.com/Pokers-1-Untold-Success/dp/1880685493)** — Ed Miller. Focuses on range thinking and how position interacts with range construction. Practical and concise.
- **[GTO Wizard Blog — Position](https://www.gtowizard.com/blog)** — Articles on IP vs OOP equity realization with solver data. The quantitative gap between IP and OOP ER is illustrated with specific hand examples and frequency charts.
