# Tips: Board Texture Analysis

## Hints

1. **Start with the pre-flop ranges, not the board.** Before assessing any texture, ask: what range would open-raise or 3-bet from this position? And what range would call? The mismatch between those two ranges against the board determines who has range advantage — and the asymmetry is often larger than intuition suggests. Players frequently underestimate how concentrated the aggressor's range is in Ax hands on an Axx board.

2. **Count the nuts, not just top pair.** On $A\heartsuit 7\clubsuit 2\diamondsuit$, both players can have $AK$ (top pair top kicker). The real asymmetry is in *sets and two pair*: who holds $77$, $22$, and $A7$? That combo asymmetry is what makes range-betting correct — not just "I have top pair so I should bet." Train yourself to quickly estimate how many set/two-pair combos each range has on each board.

3. **Ask how the turn changes this board.** Before betting the flop, visualise the distribution of 45 possible turn cards. How many are blanks that preserve your range advantage? How many complete draws and shift the advantage to the caller? On a dry board, most turns are blanks — high c-bet frequency is justified. On a wet board, many turns are dangerous — be more selective on the flop and preserve equity for favourable turns.

4. **Small bets on wet boards are the worst of both worlds.** A 25% pot bet into $J\spadesuit T\spadesuit 9\heartsuit$ gives the caller an excellent price on every draw in their range. If you bet into a wet board, commit to a size that charges draws their full equity price (60% pot or more). A probe bet that doesn't deny equity and builds the pot in a spot where your range is weaker is a common, costly leak.

5. **Balance your checking range with strong hands on wet boards.** If you always bet your best hands on wet boards, your checking range becomes capped — only weak hands remain. An attentive opponent will raise-bluff your checks without risk. Include some sets and top pair in your checking range on wet boards to keep the check-raise response credible.

## Going Deeper

- **[Applications of No-Limit Hold'em](https://www.amazon.com/Applications-No-Limit-Hold-em-Matthew-Janda/dp/1880685558)** — Matthew Janda. The definitive mathematical treatment of flop play, range betting, and polarisation. Chapters on c-betting cover range advantage and texture in depth.
- **[Modern Poker Theory](https://www.amazon.com/Modern-Poker-Theory-Building-unbeatable/dp/1909457892)** — Michael Acevedo. Solver-based framework for hand analysis; extensive sections on board texture categorisation, static vs dynamic boards, and c-betting frequency by board type.
- **[GTO Wizard Blog — Board Texture](https://www.gtowizard.com/blog)** — Articles on flop strategy, board texture categorisation, and solver-derived c-bet frequencies by board type. Look specifically for pieces on "static vs dynamic boards" and "nut advantage."
- **[Upswing Poker — Flop Strategy](https://upswingpoker.com/strategy/flop/)** — Practical breakdowns of c-betting by board type with solver output to illustrate each category. Good for concrete frequency and sizing examples.
