# Supplier Choice Is a Portfolio Decision

Single sourcing can reduce unit cost, simplify quality management, and deepen collaboration. It can also create catastrophic exposure if the supplier, region, technology, or logistics lane fails.

Dual sourcing increases optionality, but it is not free. It may reduce scale economies, require duplicate qualification, complicate engineering changes, and weaken learning curves.

## Total cost of ownership

Unit price is only one term. A serious sourcing analysis includes:

- landed cost
- minimum order quantity
- quality losses
- engineering support
- lead-time variability
- inventory required by long lead times
- currency and tariff exposure
- supplier financial health
- recovery time after disruption

## Contracts as coordination devices

Contracts allocate risk and shape behavior. Common mechanisms include:

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|---|---|
| capacity reservation | secures access before demand is final |
| take-or-pay | gives supplier demand certainty |
| volume flexibility band | allows changes within agreed limits |
| revenue sharing | aligns upside for both parties |
| indexed pricing | shares commodity or energy volatility |
| penalty and service clauses | makes reliability economically visible |

## Supplier health

A supplier under financial stress may accept an order and still fail operationally. Supplier health includes balance sheet, labor stability, quality systems, cyber posture, management depth, and dependency on a small number of customers.

## Option value

A backup supplier has option value even if it is not used every week. The option is real only when qualification, tooling, data exchange, and logistics setup are maintained. A supplier listed in a slide but unable to ship usable product is not a meaningful option.
