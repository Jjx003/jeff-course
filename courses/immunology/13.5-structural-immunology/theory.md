# Deeper theory: ensembles, energetics, and identifiability

## One sequence does not imply one immune-facing shape

Proteins occupy conformational ensembles. Loop motion, domain opening, membrane
orientation, pH, glycosylation, proteolysis, and binding partners can change which
epitopes are exposed. A predicted dominant fold is therefore not a complete model
of time-dependent immune accessibility.

For an immune claim, ask whether recognition depends on a stable surface, a rare
open state, a transient intermediate, or a modified form. The relevant experiment
may require cryo-EM, crystallography, NMR, hydrogen-deuterium exchange, crosslinking,
or kinetic measurements rather than another static prediction.

## Geometry and energetics are related but not interchangeable

A plausible interface may still bind weakly because solvent, entropy, protonation,
electrostatics, and conformational reorganization contribute to free energy.
Conversely, two complexes with similar affinity can differ in on-rate, off-rate,
force sensitivity, or geometry of receptor clustering.

This reconnects to module 3: $K_D=k_{off}/k_{on}$ summarizes an equilibrium ratio,
while immune function can depend on the separate rates and on multivalent cellular
organization.

## Structural non-identifiability

Different mechanisms can fit the same apparent structural result. Loss of binding
after mutation may reflect direct contact loss, local rearrangement, global
misfolding, altered modification, or reduced expression. The discriminating design
uses multiple mutations, expression and fold controls, orthogonal binding methods,
and functional rescue.

The principle matches the rest of the course: a measurement becomes mechanistic
when an intervention distinguishes plausible causal paths.

## Design is multi-objective

An immune binder may need affinity, specificity, breadth, solubility, stability,
low aggregation, manufacturability, suitable Fc behavior, and low immunogenicity.
Optimizing one property can damage another. Sequence models, structure predictors,
and inverse-folding tools can prioritize candidates, but the objective function
must be tied to the intended immune product and tested iteratively.
