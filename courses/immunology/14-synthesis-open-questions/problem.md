# Synthesis: make one defensible intervention

Immunology becomes coherent when every explanation names five coordinates:
**recognition, context, compartment, time, and control**. The same coordinates
explain why a vaccine protects the lung but not the nose, a tumor escapes in one
lesion, or a cell therapy clears disease while damaging a healthy lineage.

These are the coordinates introduced with immune architecture and rehearsed at
both cumulative checkpoints. The capstone adds one final demand: connect them to
a claim whose evidence level and next decision are explicit.

## Five ideas to carry forward

1. Recognition is conditional on costimulation, state, and location.
2. Architecture changes encounter probability and therefore function.
3. Diverse cross-reactive repertoires require active tolerance.
4. Responses are trajectories, not peak measurements.
5. Interventions alter selection in pathogens, tumors, and immune clones.

## A claim ladder

| Level | Claim | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| description | X is present in Y | representative measurement |
| prediction | X forecasts Y | locked held-out validation |
| mechanism | changing X changes Y through pathway M | perturbation, controls, rescue |
| decision utility | using X improves outcomes | prospective comparison with current practice |

Much frontier work legitimately reaches level one or two. The error is narrating
level three or four as though it were already measured.

## Open questions framed as decisions

- Can antigen-specific tolerance suppress the pathogenic clone while preserving
  vaccine responses and infection defense?
- Which tissue-resident states disappear before clinical improvement and return
  before relapse?
- Can receptor prediction enrich a wet-lab screen enough to change its size and
  cost on unseen antigens and HLA alleles?
- When does B-cell depletion produce temporary interruption versus durable
  repertoire reorganization?
- Which spatial feature is stable across biopsy regions, laboratories, and time?
- Can advanced products be simplified without losing the mechanism that works?

## Capstone: a four-page evidence-to-experiment brief

Choose one narrow problem in next-generation vaccination, tumor resistance,
cell therapy for autoimmunity, structural immunology, or computational immune prediction. The deliverable
is four pages plus one figure and one evidence table. A focused brief is more
useful than an encyclopedic review.

### Page 1: decision and mechanism

Define one population, present decision, failure mode, relevant tissue, and time
window. Draw a mechanism with an intervention point and at least one competing
explanation.

### Pages 2-3: evidence audit

Compare three primary studies, ideally using different evidence types. Build a
table with population/model, sample location, intervention or exposure,
comparator, time points, endpoint, effect size, causal level, and main limitation.
Do not average animal perturbation, retrospective prediction, and randomized
human evidence into one undifferentiated conclusion.

### Page 4: next experiment

Specify:

- cohort or model and feasible sample size rationale;
- intervention, comparator, and one rescue or orthogonal control;
- one primary clinical/functional endpoint and two mechanistic readouts;
- tissue and sampling times;
- a falsifying result;
- one important safety or access constraint;
- a simple analysis plan fixed before outcomes are inspected.

### Figure requirement

Use Mermaid, SVG, or a plotted public dataset. Solid arrows should represent
supported links; dashed arrows should represent hypotheses. Mark measured
variables, intervention, comparator, and failure point. Decorative art does not
count as a scientific figure.

### Practical scope example

**Question:** Does a nasal booster add durable upper-airway protection beyond a
systemic booster in previously vaccinated adults?

**Feasible design:** randomize two booster routes; measure symptomatic infection
as the clinical endpoint, nasal neutralization and resident-cell signatures at
baseline/day 14/month 3 as mechanistic readouts, and serum neutralization as a
shared comparator. A failure to improve nasal immunity despite equal systemic
responses falsifies the proposed route mechanism more directly than another
large discovery-omics panel.

## Triangulate without pretending methods are interchangeable

Human genetics can support causal relevance over a lifetime but not drug timing.
Animal models permit controlled perturbation but may misrepresent human tissue.
Trials estimate intervention effects but often sample mechanism sparsely.
Confidence rises when methods with different biases converge on the same narrow
claim.

## Final synthesis

A rigorous immune argument names who, where, when, what receptor or pathway, and
what intervention distinguishes alternatives. That discipline is more durable
than memorizing a frontier catalog, and it turns an exciting association into a
testable next decision.
