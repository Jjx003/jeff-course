# Deeper theory: measuring protection over time

## Piecewise growth is a first model, not a cell-fate theory

If net rate $g$ is approximately constant over an interval,

$$N(t)=N_0e^{gt}.$$

$N_0$ is the starting population, $N(t)$ is the population after time $t$, and
$g$ is the net per-capita rate over that interval. Real responses violate that assumption: recruitment is asynchronous, antigen
changes, divisions slow, cells migrate between sampled compartments, and
subpopulations have different death rates. Fit separate phases and interpret
the rate as a summary, not a molecular parameter.

## Antibody decay can expose multiple sources

A single exponential model is

$$A(t)=A_0e^{-kt}, \qquad t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln2}{k}.$$

Here $A_0$ is the starting measured antibody level, $A(t)$ is the level after
time $t$, $k$ is the apparent decay rate, and $t_{1/2}$ is the time required for
the measured level to halve under this model. Observed titers often show a fast early decline and a slower later phase,
consistent with contributions from short-lived and long-lived secreting cells.
Antibody half-life, ongoing secretion, tissue distribution, and assay threshold
all shape the curve.

## A correlate need not be the sole cause

A neutralizing-antibody titer can predict protection because antibody is causal,
because it covaries with other immune features, or both. Distinguish:

- **correlate:** empirically predicts outcome;
- **mechanistic correlate:** participates causally;
- **surrogate endpoint:** validated to substitute for a clinical outcome in a
  defined setting.

Intervention, depletion, passive transfer, and convergent human evidence can
strengthen a causal claim.

## Residence requires stronger evidence than a marker

A marker associated with tissue-resident memory is not proof that a cell never
recirculates. Strong evidence can include longitudinal tissue sampling,
parabiosis or transplantation models, in situ localization, clonal tracking,
and exclusion from vascular labeling, depending on species and study design.

## Original antigenic experience

Prior exposure changes precursor frequencies, antibodies, antigen availability,
and competitive access to helper signals. To detect recall bias, compare clonal
lineages and epitope targeting before and after exposure rather than comparing
only total response magnitude.
