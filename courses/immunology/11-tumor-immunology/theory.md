# Deeper theory: competing clones and survival curves

For sensitive and resistant populations,

$$N_s(t)=N_s(0)e^{r_s t},\qquad N_r(t)=N_r(0)e^{r_r t}.$$

$N_s(t)$ and $N_r(t)$ are the sensitive and resistant cell counts at time $t$;
$N_s(0)$ and $N_r(0)$ are their starting counts; and $r_s$ and $r_r$ are their
net per-capita growth rates under the current treatment. Under treatment, $r_s$ may become negative while $r_r$ remains positive. Time
to regrowth depends on both the resistant growth rate and its starting frequency.
Longitudinal tissue or circulating-DNA measurements can help distinguish
selection of a pre-existing branch from a newly acquired alteration.

## Clonal neoantigens

A useful target should be expressed and presented across most malignant cells.
A subclonal target can produce a dramatic initial response while leaving a ready
reservoir. "Neoantigen quality" is a chain of uncertain probabilities, not an
HLA-binding score alone.

## Why medians can mislead

If a minority has durable benefit, the median survival can move little while the
tail changes substantially. Inspect numbers at risk, landmark survival,
restricted mean survival time, subsequent therapies, and whether proportional
hazards is plausible.
