# Deeper theory: repertoire selection and information bottlenecks

## Presentation compresses a proteome

A cell contains many thousands of protein species but displays only a sampled
set of peptide-MHC complexes. The surface report is shaped by protein abundance,
turnover, protease preferences, transport, MHC-binding motifs, and complex
stability. Immunopeptidomics measures this report, not the proteome directly.

Presentation is a chain of filters: source-protein abundance and turnover,
peptide generation, transport, MHC loading, and surface stability. Multiplying
these into a single score would be convenient, but the steps are not independent
and are rarely measured on a common scale. Keep the causal chain instead. A
low-abundance protein can still yield a prominent peptide; an abundant protein
can be nearly invisible if processing or MHC binding is unfavorable.

## Thymic selection creates conditional usefulness

T cells must recognize self MHC weakly enough to use it as a display platform
but not react dangerously to self peptide-MHC encountered during selection.
Positive and negative selection shape this conditional repertoire. They reduce,
but do not eliminate, self-reactivity; peripheral regulation remains necessary.

## Potency depends on more than affinity

T-cell responses depend on peptide-MHC density, TCR binding kinetics, coreceptor,
adhesion, phosphatase balance, costimulation, prior differentiation, and contact
duration. A tetramer-positive cell is antigen-specific by one assay, but its
ability to proliferate, secrete cytokines, or kill must be measured separately.

## Cross-presentation solves a bootstrapping problem

A virus may infect cells that cannot travel to lymph nodes or costimulate naive
T cells. Dendritic-cell cross-presentation carries evidence from those cells
into the class I priming pathway. The activated descendants can then recognize
the same peptide-MHC I combination on infected peripheral targets.
