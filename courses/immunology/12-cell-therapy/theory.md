# Deeper theory: activation windows and logic gates

A simple Hill model describes activation versus antigen density $A$:

$$R(A)=R_{max}\frac{A^n}{K_A^n+A^n}.$$

$R(A)$ is the measured response at antigen density $A$, $R_{max}$ is the response
ceiling in that assay, $K_A$ is the density producing half-maximal response, and
$n$ controls steepness. This has the same saturating shape as antibody occupancy,
but it summarizes a whole-cell response rather than a single binding equilibrium.
Engineering that
lowers $K_A$ can improve recognition of dim disease cells while activating
against low-density healthy tissue. The useful window is between healthy- and
disease-cell antigen distributions, not between "negative" and "positive."

## Logic is analog in cells

An AND gate may require signals $S_1$ and $S_2$, but leak, unequal expression,
and tissue-dependent density blur Boolean behavior. Test a matrix of both antigen
densities, including primary healthy cells. An OR gate reduces single-antigen
escape but broadens collateral-target possibilities.

## Expansion is a confounded biomarker

Peak CAR copies can associate with response and toxicity because antigen burden
drives both expansion and clinical risk. To infer product potency, adjust for
baseline burden and conditioning, and compare functional state, not copies alone.

## Evidence progression

Case series can reveal large effects and uncommon hazards, but comparative claims
need prospective enrollment, standardized remission definitions, controls, long
follow-up, and registries capable of finding late malignancy, infection, and
reproductive outcomes.
