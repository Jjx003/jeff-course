# Study guide

## Draw it from a blank page

Sketch tissue, afferent lymph, node, efferent lymph, blood, marrow, thymus, and
spleen. Add arrows only after naming what each arrow carries. If you cannot say
whether an arrow carries antigen, a cell, or an effector molecule, the route is
not yet clear.

## Retrieval prompts

- Which organ samples blood rather than tissue lymph?
- Why does a lymph node improve both sensitivity and restraint?
- What changes between a naive T cell and its effector descendants even though
  receptor specificity is preserved?
- Why does random receptor generation make tolerance unavoidable?

## Common traps

- "Innate" does not mean weak or nonspecific.
- A lineage tree is not a map of adult cell location.
- Lymph does not flow from a node back to infected tissue directly; it rejoins
  blood first.
- Antigen, pathogen, and immunogen are not synonyms.

## Productive extension

Compare a skin infection with bacteria injected directly into blood. Keep the
microbe constant and change only the entry compartment. Predict the first
secondary lymphoid organ involved and the route antigen takes to reach it.
