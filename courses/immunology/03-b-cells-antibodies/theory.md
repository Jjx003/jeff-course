# Deeper theory: repertoire evidence and binding models

## Binding thermodynamics and kinetics

For antibody binding site $A$, ligand $B$, and bound complex $AB$,

$$K_D=\frac{[A][B]}{[AB]}=\frac{k_{off}}{k_{on}}.$$

$k_{on}$ is the association rate constant and $k_{off}$ is the dissociation rate
constant. A lower $K_D$ means tighter equilibrium binding. Two interactions with equal
$K_D$ can reach equilibrium and release antigen on different timescales. In a
flowing mucosal environment or a short receptor-entry window, kinetics can
matter independently of equilibrium occupancy.

## Diversity is sampled, not exhaustive

The theoretical sequence space is vastly larger than the number of B cells in
one body. The realized repertoire is shaped by rearrangement biases,
nonproductive joins, self-reactivity checkpoints, age, infection history, and
tissue. Cross-reactivity is therefore necessary: useful coverage cannot mean
one unique clone for every possible molecular surface.

## Reading a clonal tree

A repertoire lineage tree is an inference from sampled sequences. Internal
ancestors may be reconstructed, tissue sampling is sparse, and mutation count
is not a direct affinity measurement. Strong claims combine:

- paired heavy and light chains;
- recombinant expression of inferred antibodies;
- binding kinetics and functional assays;
- structural or escape evidence;
- longitudinal samples from relevant compartments.

## Selection can trade breadth for peak affinity

Germinal-center selection rewards a combination of antigen capture, access to
helper-cell signals, survival, and proliferative fitness while penalizing traits
such as self-reactivity or poor receptor expression. These contributions are not
known fixed weights, so a linear score would imply more precision than the biology
supports. The useful prediction is qualitative: changing antigen concentration,
epitope display, or helper availability changes which clones win. "Affinity
maturation" is real, but affinity is not the only trait under selection.
