# Consult card: immunodeficiency

Use this order every time: **organism**, **site**, **age at onset**, **family history**,
**anatomy/exposure**, **medications**, then laboratory localization.

- Separate cell count from cell function.
- Use CH50 and AH50 as a paired pathway experiment.
- In infants, an absolute lymphocyte count can be an urgent clue even before subsets return.
- Treat recurrent Neisseria as a terminal-complement clue, then confirm it.
- For HIV, keep suppression, remission, eradication, and prevention distinct.
- For targeted drugs, predict the infection and vaccine defect from the target.
- Do not call aging global immunosuppression; identify the remodeled compartment.

Common trap: ordering a giant panel without a branching hypothesis produces more
incidental abnormalities, not necessarily more understanding.
