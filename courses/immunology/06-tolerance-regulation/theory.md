# Deeper theory: tolerance as a decision problem

Central tolerance is better understood as an overlapping distribution than as a
single affinity cutoff. Increasing the strength, duration, or frequency of self
peptide-MHC encounters generally makes deletion or regulatory diversion more
likely, but antigen abundance, presenting cell, developmental state, and cytokines
also matter. Tightening selection removes more dangerous clones and also more
potentially useful cross-reactive clones. That tradeoff is the important result;
a logistic equation would add parameters that this course never estimates.

## Read the experiment, not just the marker

| Experiment | Result | Inference | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| remove a regulatory population | disease appears | population was necessary in that setting | depletion may affect other cells |
| add purified Tregs | disease falls | cells can suppress disease | transfer dose and recipient are artificial |
| mutate `FOXP3` | systemic autoimmunity | lineage program is biologically essential | mutation may alter multiple cell behaviors |
| block CTLA-4 | stronger activation, autoimmunity risk | inhibitory signaling restrains responses | does not identify every relevant cell source |
| antigen-specific challenge after therapy | target response absent, third-party response retained | specificity is plausible | ex vivo assay may miss tissue behavior |

## Why remission is not yet restoration

Define four distinct states:

1. **depletion:** the reactive population is temporarily absent;
2. **suppression:** it is present but pharmacologically constrained;
3. **exhaustion or dysfunction:** it cannot currently perform its effector program;
4. **tolerance:** durable, target-selective nonpathogenic behavior persists without
   continuous broad treatment.

A convincing tolerance study therefore needs target engagement, antigen-specific
function, unrelated protective responses, tissue evidence, and a drug-free time
horizon. Symptom improvement alone cannot distinguish these states.
