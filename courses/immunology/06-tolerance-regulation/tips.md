# Lab notebook: tolerance

When solving a case, write four columns: **where**, **cell intrinsic or extrinsic**,
**antigen specific or broad**, and **reversible or durable**. This prevents "immune
suppression" from becoming an explanation for everything.

Try these checks:

- Predict the phenotype of AIRE loss before naming the syndrome.
- Explain neonatal thymectomy as a missing-cell experiment, not a missing-organ fact.
- Distinguish `FOXP3+` staining from demonstrated suppressive function.
- For any proposed cure, ask what happens after withdrawal and after an unrelated vaccine.
- Sketch how lowering an activation threshold can improve pathogen defense while raising autoimmunity risk.

Common trap: anergy, exhaustion, suppression, deletion, and tolerance can all
produce a quiet assay. The mechanism and durability are different.
